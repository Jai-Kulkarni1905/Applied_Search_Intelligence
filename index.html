Can a learned model improve which pages an SEO team reviews first?

A client-grouped evaluation of a transparent ranking baseline and Random Forest using pseudonymized search-performance data.

Abstract

This study asks whether a learned model can improve the prioritization of pages for human content review beyond a transparent rule-based score. Using March 2026 pseudonymized FlyRank warehouse data, page-level features were built from 1–15 March and an observed decline outcome from 16–31 March, with client-grouped validation used to prevent pages from the same client appearing across train and test. A Random Forest ranked pages by predicted probability of subsequent impression decline, while the baseline combined position-adjusted CTR need, visibility strength, position need, and zero-visibility need. On the primary held-out client-grouped test set, the Random Forest remained below the baseline at every evaluated K, although its Precision@K stayed above the 0.49 held-out base rate; across five GroupKFold checks it also exceeded the corresponding base rate in every fold. The evidence therefore supports using the transparent baseline as the stronger decision-support queue for this dataset, while treating the learned model as evidence of directional predictive signal rather than proof that a refresh will cause recovery.

1. Introduction / Problem statement

The practical decision is not “which pages will recover?” It is which pages should a content practitioner review first?

Content and SEO teams have finite review capacity. A useful prioritization system should place pages with stronger evidence of opportunity near the top of the queue while making the reason for that ranking understandable. A poor ranking can waste editorial effort on a low-value page or delay attention to a page with meaningful search visibility.

The capstone therefore treats ranking as the decision problem. Data and ML are useful because page-level search performance combines several observable signals—visibility, clicks, CTR, position and traffic context. The learned model is tested against a deliberately transparent baseline rather than being treated as valuable simply because it is more complex.

Research questionDoes learning relationships among observable page-level signals produce a better top-K review ranking than a transparent, rule-based score?

What was excluded

  • Future impressions and outcome fields — used only to construct/evaluate the held-out outcome.
  • Trend fields such as trend_pct and trend_direction — they directly describe the performance movement being evaluated.
  • Client/content IDs as model features — retained for grouping, joins and checks, but not used as predictive signals.
  • Product-decision fields — product flags or existing decision scores were not used as model inputs.
  • Rows without available GSC data — unavailable measurement is not treated as zero performance.

The public-safe presentation uses aggregated metrics and pseudonymous references only. It does not publish client names, domains, raw URLs, private queries, titles or credentials.

3. Methodology

Baseline: a transparent action score

The baseline is designed to rank pages for review using observable first-half information. It combines four equal-weight components:

ComponentWhat it capturesHigher value means
ctr_needCTR below the expected CTR for the page's position bucketlarger CTR shortfall
visibility_strengthnormalized log1p impressionsgreater existing exposure
position_needrelative search-position weaknesspoorer position
zero_visibility_needno impressions in the information windowcomplete lack of observed visibility

Action score = CTR need + visibility strength + position need + zero-visibility need.

The baseline was evaluated only after ranking pages from the first-half information window. The later window was kept separate for the observed outcome.

Learned model

A Random Forest classifier was selected to test whether non-linear relationships and interactions among observable signals add ranking value beyond the simple rule. Its predicted probability of is_declining = 1 becomes the ranking score.

FeatureRole
impressionsSearch exposure during the information window
clicksSearch clicks during the information window

sessions_organicOrganic sessions

ctrClicks divided by impressions

Observed target

A page is labeled is_declining = 1 when its average daily impressions in 16–31 March are lower than its average daily impressions in 1–15 March. This is an observed subsequent outcome, not a label based on an existing FlyRank product decision.

Validation and leakage control

The primary model evaluation uses a 70/30 client-grouped split: 30 clients in training and 13 unseen clients in testing, with 129,698 training rows and 11,769 test rows. No client appears in both sets. The final robustness check uses five GroupKFold client-grouped folds.

Leakage check: passed.The final scoring features were constructed from 1–15 March. The 16–31 March outcome was held out for evaluation. The notebook also checked for future/outcome columns and product-decision flags in the scoring dataframe. The leakage audit was performed before model training, so it did not depend on observing model performance.

5. Limitations & honest framing

The evidence is strongest for reproducible prioritization under the evaluated conditions—not for causal recovery.

What the evidence supports

  • The workflow can produce a reproducible page-prioritization queue.
  • The Random Forest contains useful directional signal relative to the held-out base rate.
  • Client-grouped validation tests performance on unseen clients.
  • The transparent baseline is stronger than the learned model on the primary Precision@K comparison.
  • The queue can support human review and triage.

What it does not establish

  • That refreshing a selected page will cause recovery.
  • That a high-ranked page is guaranteed to improve after editing.
  • That the model predicts Google's ranking algorithm.
  • That the learned model will outperform the baseline on every future client or period.
  • That observational freshness/performance patterns are causal effects.

“A high-ranked page is a candidate for human review, not a guaranteed recovery opportunity.”

Important evaluation limits

  • The main evaluation is based on one March 2026 decision/holdout period.
  • The final modeling table contains 141,467 pages across 43 clients with observed outcomes.
  • Client-grouped validation is stronger for cross-client generalization than a row-level random split, but it does not establish performance across all future periods.
  • Observed impression decline is a useful ranking outcome for this study, but it is not the same thing as proving that a page needs a content refresh.

6. Ranked recommendations

The operational output should be used as a triage mechanism. The rank determines where limited review time starts; the reason code supplies the first diagnostic question; human judgment determines the action.

01

Use the transparent baseline as the primary queue

On the measured objective, it outperformed the Random Forest at every evaluated K. The result argues against adding model complexity without a demonstrated ranking benefit.

02

Review visible pages with a CTR shortfall first

low_ctr_visible_page indicates meaningful visibility with CTR below the position-tier benchmark. Review title/snippet presentation and search-intent alignment before editing.

03

Diagnose poor-position pages rather than assuming a CTR problem

poor_position points toward content relevance, coverage and ranking competition. A low position alone is not proof that the page is poor.

04

Defer low-evidence pages

insufficient_visibility means there is not enough search exposure for a reliable CTR-based review. position_unavailable means the position-adjusted comparison cannot be made.

05

Monitor and rerun rather than treating the queue as permanent

Rerun on a new decision/holdout period, monitor shifts in impressions, CTR and position, and re-check Precision@K when a later held-out outcome becomes available. Reconsider the model only if measured drift shows the simple baseline is no longer adequate.

Human review is mandatory before content action.Check search intent, SERP context, content quality and coverage, business importance, and possible technical or measurement issues. Do not publish, delete or materially rewrite content solely from the score.
